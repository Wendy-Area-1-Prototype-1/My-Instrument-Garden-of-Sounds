Scenario: Creativity for Senior Centres

Prompt:

Design a way for seniors to make music, and feel proud of their work.

Context:

Many different forms of creativity have been linked to positive aging outcomes. Creativity in this context shouldn't be focused on mastery or novelty, but might instead be approached as a craft and/or social activity.

Discussion:

Based on my understanding of the brief, I can identify a few notable details related to how it sets up this project's purpose and worth. At the core, like most (if not all) instruments, is the goal of using it to make music. The purpose and process of making music is not singular, so based on the angle presented in the brief, to approach it as "craft and/or social activity", I might interpret the importance of making music through performance either with or for others. This differs from other ways in which a user might understand making music, such as from a personal or compositional approach.

Taking performance as an integral aspect of the instruments purpose, I am drawn to thinking about the underlying social aspects of the act. How can performance serve to connect with others, either through direct experience of a piece of music, or perhaps drawing in shared memories of prior experiences with music culture. I can also see the dynamics of crafting communities coming into play through the potential of instruments to facilitate teaching and learning between two or more people.

Building off of this interpretation of purpose, and other aspects of the context's particulars, I can recognise its potential worth through three particular values: approachability, familiarity and Maturity. It should be approachable, as something that doesn't feel overwhelmingly complex in its initial appearance. However, this doesn't mean it should not be complex, but that this complexity is something that may be optionally revealed over time. In this way it should also be mature, something that doesn't feel like a toy, overly simple in its appearance or functionality. Finally, tying into the purpose of connecting with others through shared cultural experiences, is the value of familiarity. The instrument should draw on familiar cultural framings - either of other instruments, or other aspects of musical culture.

Synopsis

I will design an instrument focused on performing accompaniments to a collection of popular songs. This will include "play along" visual feedback for the song's instrumental parts as well as its lyrics, with optional levels of auto-accompaniment.

Response to Purpose and Worth

This responds to the purpose of allowing for performance to be a social act. By allowing for the visual feedback to address multiple musical parts (instrumental and vocal) it encourages engagement with others for that performance. In choosing which particular songs are included in its repertoire, there's opportunity to engage with the cultural specificity of the instrument's context, such as the age of the residents, and the location of the centre. I would minimise the particular controls over the tone of the instrument, instead providing a preset sound matched to the song selection, helping with the overall approachability. I would also allow for a variance in the amount of auto-accompaniment, letting users choose the volume of it in relation to their own performance (like karaoke). This would additionally encourage some aspect of learning - as someone became more familiar with the particular song they might dial down the accompaniment.

Framing

A key conceptual influence on this project is Karaoke - and what if it could be applied to the vocals and accompaniment. It's important to note that someone who is currently 70 years old would have been a teenager in the 1970s where the technology of music playback allowed for Karaoke and "sing alongs" to develop in this particular form. While Karaoke machines (and jukeboxes) have their own interesting aesthetic lineage, I would perhaps instead draw more specifically on the characterisation of something like Hammond's 1970 electric organ designs, with wood panelling and colourful toggles.

Interaction design analysis

Example 1: Virtual Piano

I chose this example to its discuss information design, in particular the way it handles searching for a song. There is lots I don't like about other aspects of the info design, but this is the most relevant for my project. When arriving at the keyboard users can select a piece of sheet music to play, not too dissimilar to my Karaoke concept. They do so by using the search box (pictured below).

Clicking on the box doesn't provide any immediate suggesting for the kinds of music sheets I might be able to search for, rather it just makes the box larger. As I start to type it gives me a few preselected categories, before it loads in the prediction of the song I'm looking for. Selecting anything from the categories takes me to a separate page, but selecting the prediction loads the song straight into the UI. This represents a discord of information design, with different ways of displaying information based on the kinds of action taken, rather than presenting all the information within a single frame.

Besides the issues with how the information is presented within the experience of interaction, there is also the selection of songs that I might choose from, with a seemingly large but also shallow pool of information. For example there's many different variations on the song "Mad World", but based on the Imagine Dragons or Gary Jules covers, rather than the original, with no way of knowing which is which until selecting the sheet.

Picking such an example is perhaps unfair, but it provides a valuable impact on what I want my project's information design not to be like. Selecting the particular song to play should be integrated within the singular instrument UI. Preferably this could be done through an initial presentation of categories, along with a search function if needed, for people to explore rather than needing to input something ahead of time. It would support this through a more curated choice of included songs - seeking to represent a smaller set more relevant to its cultural context, and also making it possible to explore more directly.
